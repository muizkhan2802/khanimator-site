In his own words

Drawing till
I drop

“Just another animator out there drawing till I drop.”

It undersells it a little. What started in 2019 as fan animations made purely for the love of it has turned into a body of 78 films, an audience of nearly 35,000 across YouTube and Instagram, and close to two million views.

The work is almost entirely fight animation — One Piece, Naruto, Jujutsu Kaisen, Demon Slayer, Chainsaw Man. Not recreations, but the fights that should have happened, or the ones that deserved better choreography than they got. Every frame is drawn by hand, and the longest single piece took a full year to finish.

Alongside the films there's a running thread of teaching: FlipaClip tutorials, breakdowns of how sound design transforms a sequence, and side-by-side comparisons of fan animation against the official adaptation. The Discord and Patreon exist for the people who want to follow the process rather than just the result.

The run so far

Six years,
frame by frame

A channel built slowly and in public — every milestone below is a video anyone can still go and watch.

  1. 2019

    The channel opens

    Khanimator goes live on 28 June, starting with short fan animations made purely for fun.

  2. 2022

    Luffy vs Kaido breaks through

    The first film to find a real audience — 119K views, and the moment the channel stops being a hobby nobody sees.

  3. 2024

    A year on one animation

    “This animation took me a year to make…” becomes the channel's biggest film at 640K views, followed by Sukuna vs Jogo at 153K.

  4. 2025

    Sound takes centre stage

    Sound design becomes a subject in its own right, and voice acting enters the films for the first time.

  5. 2026

    Five animators, one crossover

    “Buggy Solos Sukuna?” lands as the channel's largest collaboration to date — Jujutsu Kaisen against One Piece, 80K views.

How it gets made

The craft

No shortcuts, no motion-tweened cheats. Drawn frames and a lot of patience.

Frame by frame

Every drawing made individually — no rigs, no tweens. It's why a ninety-second film can take a year.

Built in FlipaClip

The main tool, used far past what it's normally asked to do — and the subject of the channel's tutorials.

Sound as a co-star

Impact, weight and timing are as much audio as they are drawing. Recent films add voice acting on top.

Built with others

The biggest projects are collaborations. Five animators on the last one, coordinated through the Discord.
